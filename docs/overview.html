Course content

The course follows chemical processes from air through water to soils and contaminants. Water chemistry receives the most quantitative depth. Each unit has notes, a short lecture, and exercises with worked solutions. Start with a chemical prediction, calculate a small case, and explain the result’s limits.

Topic sequence and reading

Chapter references are to vanLoon and Duffy, Environmental Chemistry: A Global Perspective, fourth edition (2017). The notes specify the topics to select within those chapters and what to defer; these are not whole-chapter reading requirements. Exact subsection and page assignments await book review.

Unit Book chapters Chemical focus
1. Environmental amounts and balances 1 Concentration, load, boundaries, and conservation.
2. Reactions in the atmosphere 2–4 Photolysis, ozone cycles, catalysts, and rates.
3. Atmospheric deposition and acidity 5–7 Oxidation, wet/dry transfer, and equivalent budgets.
4. Molecules, radiation, and climate 8 Infrared absorption, spectra, particles, and persistence.
5. Aquatic composition and equilibrium 9–10 Charge balance, activity, and simple speciation; activity A.
6. Carbonate and gas chemistry 10–11 Carbonate, alkalinity, gas exchange, calcite; activity B.
7. Organic matter in water 12 Binding, colour, carbon, and oxygen demand.
8. Metal speciation and mobility 13 Ligands, hydrolysis, free ions, and solubility.
9. Colloids, surfaces, and sorption 14 Surface charge, partition balances, and mobility.
10. Redox and environmental cycles 10, 13, 15 Electron balances, nitrogen, iron, sulfur, and phosphate.
11. Water pollution and treatment chemistry 16 Chemical transformations, separation, and residuals.
12. Soil chemistry and retention 17–18 Exchange charge, mineral inventories, and acid response.
13. Waste materials and leachate chemistry 19 Sulfide oxidation, acidity, and neutralisation.
14. Organic contaminants and environmental fate 20 Ionisation, partitioning, parent loss; activity C.
15. Connecting air, catchments, and water 21 and earlier A chemical argument supported by budgets and evidence.

Weekly interactive demos

Use the five weekly demos to change chemical conditions and check the consequences before returning to a hand calculation. The working study-week grouping is atmosphere and balances; aquatic composition and carbonate; metals and surfaces; soils and contaminants; then synthesis. The pages link back to the relevant units and include static reference results.

Three supporting computing activities

Use the same R and R Markdown workflow as introductory computing. These activities support chemical interpretation; no new statistical methods are introduced. The site uses Quarto, while student reports remain .Rmd files.

Activity Files Result
A: water analysis Download ZIP An ion table, charge checks, and a documented correction
B: carbonate species Download ZIP A hand-checked distribution curve at prescribed pH
C: contaminant fate Download ZIP A two-phase parent balance and reproducible chemical explanation

Extract an activity ZIP into its own folder, open that folder as the working project, and keep its report next to its script and data. Activities A and C use readr, dplyr, and ggplot2; B uses ggplot2. All reports use rmarkdown. Use the package installation workflow from introductory computing if needed. The scripts and data are also available individually from the activity pages.

Reading reference

The publisher’s contents provide the book sequence. Sources linked in each unit support its chemical concepts; the supplied fictitious compounds and constructed datasets are identified explicitly. The course does not use invented results as field evidence.
